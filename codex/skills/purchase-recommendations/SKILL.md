# Purchase recommendations

Recommend an offering whose suitability is supported by evidence. Make
uncertainty visible before the user spends money, rather than after purchase.

## Establish the requirements

Identify the intended outcome, essential requirements, budget, and relevant
constraints from the conversation. Ask only for missing details that could
change the recommendation. Depending on the purchase, these may include size,
compatibility, accessibility, location, timing, ingredients, ownership costs,
or cancellation terms. Do not turn every purchase into a questionnaire.

Distinguish requirements from preferences and nice-to-have features. Preserve
the user's chosen category and priorities; explain any proposed substitution.
Evaluate suitability for this user's use case, rather than treating popularity,
a high price, or a long feature list as proof of quality.

Use the requested delivery or service location and currency. Seller location
is a separate consideration: overseas offers are eligible if they serve the
user's destination and meet their requirements. Do not assume a country or
restrict sellers geographically unless the user asks or a practical constraint
requires it.

## Verify the exact offering

Browse current sources before recommending a purchase. Open the actual offer
page; search snippets and category pages are leads, not sufficient evidence.
Match the exact variant, edition, package, service tier, or booking option.
Check quantities, inclusions, exclusions, condition, and seller identity where
they affect the decision. A description of a product family or premium tier
may not apply to the specific offer being recommended.

Use authoritative sources for factual capabilities and terms: manufacturer
specifications, ingredient labels, provider documentation, or official policies
as appropriate. Use the seller's exact listing for the offered variant, price,
availability, and fulfilment. Seek independent reviews or other evidence for
experiential claims such as comfort, durability, or service quality; distinguish
those assessments from measurable facts and marketing promises.

Check the details that determine whether the purchase meets the requirements.
Examples include clothing measurements and return eligibility, furniture
clearance and assembly, food ingredients and quantity, software platform and
licensing restrictions, or a service's coverage and cancellation policy.
For hardware, verify compatibility and explicit support for required functions;
physical fit or a familiar connector does not establish capability.
Use only the checks relevant to the purchase.

Distinguish what the user has confirmed, what a source establishes, and what
appearance or inference merely suggests. An unclear photograph cannot resolve
a critical detail; prefer an original image, label, exact identifier, or
measurement over repeated inconclusive captures.

When sources disagree, state the conflict and resolve the relevant detail
before calling an offer suitable. Do not silently choose the source supporting
the initial guess. If verification remains blocked, give a conditional candidate
and the precise unresolved requirement rather than a confident recommendation.

## Compare real cost and practical terms

Compare costs over a period relevant to the user's intended use. Include
mandatory fees, delivery, taxes or potential import charges, required extras,
consumables, recurring charges, and renewal prices when material. Distinguish
verified totals from estimates and costs that depend on checkout or location.
An introductory subscription price is not its ongoing cost.

Check material fulfilment and after-purchase terms: destination eligibility,
estimated delivery or service date, returns, warranty, cancellation, and refund
restrictions. For international offers, consider return shipping and regional
support or usage restrictions. Do not imply guaranteed arrival or universal
consumer protections without evidence.

Assess seller credibility when it materially changes the risk. Do not treat
marketplace ratings, sponsored rankings, or affiliate recommendations alone
as proof of authenticity, reliability, or suitability.

## Present a usable recommendation

Lead with the best-supported choice and a direct offer link. Explain how it
meets the user's priorities, cite the evidence supporting decisive claims, and
state meaningful limitations. Include the exact identifier or option when useful
so the user can recognise substitutions or listing changes.

Report price and availability only when actually verified; mark missing or
unclear values accordingly. Do not treat dynamic-page placeholders as confirmed
stock. Separate current facts, subjective judgments, and unresolved uncertainty.
Do not guarantee suitability beyond the available evidence.

Keep the shortlist proportional to the request. Offer alternatives for meaningful
tradeoffs, not to fill an arbitrary quota. Explain value in terms of the user's
needs rather than automatically choosing the cheapest or most expensive option.

If challenged, recheck the exact evidence and answer with that evidence.
Correct earlier mistakes plainly; confidence is not a source. Suggest a simple
acceptance check when useful, without claiming it has already succeeded.
